Greedy algorithm is a common approach to solving optimization problems. Its basic idea is to choose the option that appears best at each decision stage, that is, to greedily make locally optimal decisions in the hope of obtaining a globally optimal solution. Greedy algorithms are simple and efficient, and are widely used in many practical problems.

- Dynamic programming considers all previous decisions when making the current decision, and uses solutions to past subproblems to construct the solution to the current subproblem.
- Greedy algorithms do not consider past decisions, but instead make greedy choices moving forward, continually reducing the problem size until the problem is solved.


In general, greedy algorithms are applicable in the following two situations.

1. **The optimal solution can be guaranteed**: In this case, greedy algorithms are often the best choice because they tend to be more efficient other algorithm solutions
2. **An approximately optimal solution can be found**: For many complex problems, finding the global optimal solution is very difficult, so efficiently finding a suboptimal solution is already a very good outcome.


But there's also 2 conditions that apply to the problem when using greedy algorithms:

- **Greedy choice property**: Only when locally optimal choices can always lead to a globally optimal solution can greedy algorithms guarantee obtaining the optimal solution.
- **Optimal substructure**: The optimal solution to the original problem contains the optimal solutions to subproblems.

---

## How to Greedy

To use a greedy algorithm, we first identify the objective and constraints. This is necessary, but it does not tell us whether greedy is suitable.

1. Decide what looks best at each step.
    - Define a rule for making the locally best choice. We commit to that choice, reduce the remaining problem, and repeat without reconsidering earlier decisions.
    
2. Check whether the choice is safe.
    - Making progress toward a solution is not enough. We must ask whether committing to this choice preserves the possibility of an optimal overall solution. A choice can help immediately but lead to a worse final result.
    
3. Justify why the strategy works.
    - The greedy-choice property means that at least one optimal solution includes our chosen first move. Optimal substructure means that solving the remaining subproblem optimally then completes an optimal solution to the original problem.

If a counterexample breaks the rule, that particular greedy strategy is incorrect. We must revise the strategy or consider another approach.
