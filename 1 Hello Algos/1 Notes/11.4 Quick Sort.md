Quick sort is a sorting algorithm that uses the concept of divide and conquer.


Its core operation is “partitioning”: choose an array element as the “pivot,” move all smaller elements to its left, and move all larger elements to its right. The partitioning process is shown below.

1. Choose an element as the pivot based on a heuristic, then initialize pointers `i` and `j` at the two ends of the array.
2. In a loop, use `i` to find the first element greater than the pivot and `j` to find the first element smaller than it, then swap those elements.
3. Repeat step `2` until `i` and `j` meet. Finally, swap the pivot into the boundary between the two subarrays.

What is this heuristic? There are a few common ways to choose the pivot.

- **Pick the leftmost or rightmost element** in the current subarray.
- **Pick a random element** by choosing an index uniformly at random from the current subarray.
- **Use the median of three.** Look at the first, middle, and last elements, then choose the one with the middle value.

The pivot matters because it determines **how much work is left after each partition**. Each partition scans the current subarray once, so it takes linear time in the size of that subarray.
- **If the pivot is near the median**, the two subarrays are roughly the same size. Each level of recursion does at most $O(n)$ work in total, and the subarrays shrink geometrically over $O(\log n)$ levels. This gives $\Theta(n \log n)$ time.
- **If the pivot keeps being the smallest or largest element**, one subarray is empty and the other shrinks by just one element. The work adds up to $n + (n-1) + \cdots + 1$, giving $\Theta(n^2)$ time.

With distinct elements, choosing a random pivot at each step gives an expected running time of $\Theta(n \log n)$.

---

After partitioning, the original array has three parts: the left subarray, the pivot, and the right subarray. Every element in the left subarray is  the pivot, which is  every element in the right subarray. We now need only sort the two subarrays.

But the partitioning continues... and it repeats a cycle:

1. Partition the original array to obtain unsorted left and right subarrays.
2. Recursively partition the left and right subarrays.
3. Continue until each subarray has length 1, completing the sort.
